How to use carbon intensity to cut emissions for free

Grid intensity changes every half hour as wind, sun, demand and imports shift. On a windy night in Britain it can drop below 50 g/kWh; on a still winter evening it can top 250 g/kWh. Moving flexible loads — EV charging, dishwashers, laundry, water heating and home-battery charging — into the cleanest window can cut the emissions of those loads by 30–60% without using a single kWh less.

  1. Automate it. Most EVs, smart plugs and heat-pump controllers let you schedule by time; many integrate carbon-aware charging directly.
  2. Pair with a time-of-use tariff. Low-carbon hours are often the cheapest hours too.
  3. Add storage. A home battery lets you charge when the grid is clean and run on stored power at the dirty peak. Get battery quotes.
