Frequently asked questions

Where does Carbon.Energy get its data?

Grid intensity and emissions come from Our World in Data's open energy and CO₂ datasets (built on Ember, the Energy Institute and the Global Carbon Project). Atmospheric CO₂ comes from NOAA's Mauna Loa record, live British grid data from the National Energy System Operator Carbon Intensity API, and sunshine data from Open-Meteo. An automated job refreshes the data on a schedule.

Are the calculators accurate?

They use published emission factors and your own inputs, so they're good for decisions and comparisons, typically within ±15–25% of a professional assessment. For investment-grade numbers, request a site survey through our free quote service.

How do free quotes work?

Tell us what you're considering and where. We match you with up to three vetted installers or consultants who serve your area. There's no cost and no obligation, and we never sell your details to unrelated marketers.

Is Carbon.Energy affiliated with an energy company?

No. Carbon.Energy is an independent, ad-supported information service. See our disclaimer and trademark notice.
