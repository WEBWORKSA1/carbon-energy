Why parts per million matters

Carbon dioxide is a small fraction of the atmosphere — a little over 0.04% — but it traps heat very effectively. Before the industrial revolution it hovered around 280 ppm for thousands of years. Burning coal, oil and gas, making cement and clearing forests have pushed it past 420 ppm, a level the planet last saw millions of years ago. Because CO₂ lingers for centuries, the curve only flattens when global emissions fall close to net zero; cutting emissions slows the rise, it doesn't immediately reverse it.

That's also why durable carbon removal is getting attention alongside cutting emissions: reaching net zero means balancing any remaining emissions with CO₂ physically taken back out of the air.
