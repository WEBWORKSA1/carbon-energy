Emissions

CO₂ (carbon dioxide)
The main long-lived greenhouse gas from burning fossil fuels and making cement. Stays in the atmosphere for centuries.
CO₂e (carbon dioxide equivalent)
A common unit that converts other gases — methane, nitrous oxide, F-gases — into the amount of CO₂ that would cause the same warming over a set period (usually 100 years, GWP100).
ppm (parts per million)
How CO₂ concentration in air is measured. 420 ppm means 420 CO₂ molecules per million molecules of dry air. See the CO₂ tracker.
Carbon footprint
Total greenhouse gas emissions caused by a person, product or organisation over a period, usually expressed in tonnes of CO₂e per year.
Life-cycle assessment (LCA)
Accounting for emissions across a product's whole life — materials, manufacturing, use and disposal.
Radiative forcing (RF) / non-CO₂ effects
Extra warming from aviation contrails and NOₓ at altitude, often approximated by multiplying flight CO₂ by around 1.7–1.9.
Methane (CH₄)
A potent, shorter-lived greenhouse gas from gas leaks, agriculture and landfills — roughly 80 times stronger than CO₂ over 20 years.

Electricity

Carbon intensity (gCO₂/kWh)
Grams of CO₂ (or CO₂e) emitted per kilowatt-hour of electricity. Below 100 is very clean; above 600 is coal-heavy. Full guide.
kW vs kWh
kW is power (a rate, like speed); kWh is energy (an amount, like distance). A 2 kW heater running for 3 hours uses 6 kWh.
Capacity factor
Actual output as a share of the maximum possible. Solar is typically 10–25%, onshore wind 25–40%, nuclear 90%+.
Baseload / peak load
The minimum steady demand on a grid versus the highest demand periods, usually early evening.
Low-carbon electricity
Renewables plus nuclear power.
Marginal emissions
The emissions of the power plant that responds when demand changes — often gas or coal — which can differ from the average intensity.
Curtailment
When wind or solar output is deliberately reduced because the grid can't absorb it.
Time-of-use (TOU) tariff
Electricity pricing that varies by time of day, rewarding flexible use.

Home technology

Photovoltaic (PV)
Technology that converts sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor cells.
kWp (kilowatt-peak)
A solar system's rated output under standard test conditions. A 6 kWp system has, for example, fourteen 430 W panels.
Peak sun hours
Daily solar energy expressed as hours of 1,000 W/m² sunlight. Equals kWh/m²/day.
Performance ratio
Share of theoretical output a real system delivers after heat, wiring, inverter and soiling losses — typically 75–85%.
Net metering / export tariff
How you're credited for solar power sent to the grid. Full net metering credits at retail price; many regions now pay less.
Self-consumption
The share of your solar output used on site. Higher self-consumption usually means faster payback.
Heat pump
A device that moves heat rather than creating it, delivering 2.5–4.5 units of heat per unit of electricity.
COP / SCOP
Coefficient of performance (at one moment) and seasonal COP (averaged across a year). SCOP 3.2 = 3.2 kWh of heat per kWh of electricity.
Heat-loss calculation
Room-by-room estimate of how much heat a home loses, used to size a heat pump correctly.
Level 2 / 7 kW charger
A dedicated home EV charger, typically adding 40–60 km of range per hour.
V2H / V2G
Vehicle-to-home and vehicle-to-grid: using an EV's battery to power a house or support the grid.

Carbon markets

Carbon credit / offset
A certificate representing one tonne of CO₂e avoided, reduced or removed by a project, which a buyer can use to compensate for emissions. Guide.
Carbon removal (CDR)
Taking CO₂ out of the atmosphere and storing it — e.g. reforestation, biochar, enhanced weathering, direct air capture.
Additionality
Whether the climate benefit would have happened anyway without credit revenue. Non-additional credits don't change the atmosphere.
Permanence
How long stored carbon stays out of the atmosphere — decades for some forests, millennia for geological storage.
Compliance vs voluntary market
Compliance markets (like the EU ETS) are set by law with emission caps; voluntary markets are used by companies and individuals by choice.
Carbon price
A cost per tonne of CO₂ set by a tax or cap-and-trade scheme.

Business & policy

Scope 1, 2, 3
Direct emissions; purchased energy; value-chain emissions. Guide.
GHG Protocol
The most widely used global standard for corporate greenhouse gas accounting.
Net zero
Cutting emissions as close to zero as possible and balancing the remainder with durable removals.
SBTi
The Science Based Targets initiative, which validates corporate emission-reduction targets.
PPA (power purchase agreement)
A long-term contract to buy electricity directly from a generator, often a wind or solar farm.
REC / GO
Renewable energy certificates (North America) or Guarantees of Origin (Europe) documenting 1 MWh of renewable generation.
CSRD / ISSB
Sustainability reporting frameworks in the EU (Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive) and internationally (IFRS S1/S2).
