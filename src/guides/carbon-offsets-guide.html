A carbon credit represents one tonne of CO₂-equivalent that a project claims to have avoided, reduced or removed from the atmosphere. Buyers retire credits to compensate for emissions they haven't yet eliminated. Used carefully, credits channel money into real climate projects. Used carelessly, they become an expensive permission slip — investigative reporting and academic studies have found that some popular credit types delivered far less than claimed.

Avoidance vs removal

Avoidance / reduction creditsRemoval credits
What they doPrevent emissions that would otherwise happenTake CO₂ already in the air and store it
ExamplesProtecting forests, clean cookstoves, landfill gas capture, renewable projectsReforestation, soil carbon, biochar, enhanced weathering, direct air capture with storage
Typical price rangeA few dollars to ~$30/t~$20/t (nature-based) to several hundred dollars per tonne (engineered, durable)
Role in net zeroUseful finance for transition; can't neutralise emissions on their ownRequired to balance residual emissions at net zero

Five quality tests

  1. Additionality: would the project have happened anyway? Renewable projects in markets where wind and solar are already the cheapest option often fail this test.
  2. Baseline realism: are the "would have happened" emissions credible, or inflated? Several forest-protection programmes have been criticised for exaggerated deforestation baselines.
  3. Permanence: how long is the carbon stored? Trees can burn; geological storage and mineralisation can last millennia.
  4. Leakage: does protecting one forest just push logging next door?
  5. Verification and registry: is the project certified under a recognised standard, assessed against frameworks such as the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market's Core Carbon Principles, and are credits serialised and retired publicly so they can't be double-counted?

How to use credits credibly

  • Reduce first. Credits should cover what you can't cut yet, not replace cutting. Start with the footprint calculator or the business estimator.
  • Prefer "contribution" claims ("we funded X tonnes of removal") over blanket "carbon neutral" labels, which regulators in several jurisdictions are scrutinising.
  • Shift toward durable removals over time, even if you start with a blended portfolio.
  • Disclose what you bought: project type, standard, vintage, registry IDs and price.

Budgeting

For planning, multiply your residual tonnes by a range: roughly $15/t for higher-quality nature-based credits and $150–$600/t for durable engineered removal. Our CO₂ equivalents tool does this automatically. Prices vary widely by project and vintage, so treat these as ballpark figures.
