Two solar quotes for "the same" system can differ by thousands of dollars — and the cheaper one isn't always the better deal. Put each quote into the same simple table and check these twelve points.

Price

  1. Cost per watt. Divide the total price (before incentives) by system size in watts. This is the only fair way to compare different-sized systems.
  2. Cost per expected kWh. Divide the price by 25 years of estimated production. This rewards systems designed for your roof rather than just bigger ones.
  3. What's included. Permits, interconnection fees, monitoring, critter guards, electrical panel upgrades and roof work are frequent hidden extras.

Production

  1. Annual kWh estimate. Ask how it was modelled (software, shading analysis, orientation). Compare against our independent estimate; a quote that's more than 15% higher deserves questions.
  2. Shading analysis. A good installer measures shade on site or with high-resolution imagery, not guesswork.
  3. Self-consumption assumption. Savings depend on how much you use directly versus export. Make sure the quote states the export rate it assumed.

Equipment

  1. Panels: manufacturer, model, wattage, efficiency and degradation warranty (look for ~85%+ output after 25 years).
  2. Inverter: string inverter, optimisers or microinverters? Microinverters and optimisers handle partial shade better; string inverters are cheaper and simpler.
  3. Battery (if quoted): usable kWh, power (kW), round-trip efficiency, warranty years and throughput.

Protection

  1. Workmanship warranty: 10 years or more is a sign of confidence. Who honours it if the installer closes?
  2. Licences, insurance and certifications required in your region — ask for numbers and check them.
  3. Financing terms: for loans, compare APR and total paid, not monthly payment. For leases or PPAs, check escalators, transfer rules if you sell your home, and buy-out terms.

Red flags

  • "Today-only" discounts or pressure to sign at the kitchen table.
  • Savings projections with no electricity price or export rate stated.
  • Incentives promised that you can't verify on an official site.
  • No written production estimate.
