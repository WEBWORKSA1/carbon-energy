Large companies now have to report emissions across their value chains, which means their suppliers — small and mid-sized businesses — are increasingly asked for carbon data in tenders, procurement questionnaires and loan applications. The good news: a solid first inventory is a data-gathering exercise, not a research project. Most SMEs can produce one in a week.

The three scopes

ScopeWhat it coversTypical SME sources
1 — DirectFuel you burn and gases you releaseGas boilers, company vehicles, generators, refrigerant leaks
2 — Purchased energyElectricity, heat, steam and cooling you buyOffice, warehouse and shop electricity
3 — Value chainEverything else, upstream and downstreamPurchased goods and services, business travel, commuting, waste, freight, use of sold products

For service businesses, Scope 3 is often 70–90% of the total. That's why customer questionnaires focus on it — and why it's worth getting roughly right rather than perfectly wrong.

Step 1 — Set boundaries

Pick a 12-month reporting period (usually your financial year) and decide which sites and entities are in scope. The GHG Protocol's "operational control" approach — include what you run — is simplest for most SMEs.

Step 2 — Gather activity data

  • Energy: 12 months of electricity and gas bills (kWh, not just dollars).
  • Vehicles: fuel-card litres or mileage logs by fuel type.
  • Refrigerants: service records showing top-ups.
  • Travel: booking-platform exports (flights by distance, hotel nights).
  • Commuting: a five-question staff survey — distance, mode, days in office.
  • Purchases: accounts-payable spend by category for the year.
  • Waste: tonnes by stream from your hauler.

Step 3 — Apply emission factors

Multiply each activity by an emission factor. Use your national government's published factors where available, the IEA or Ember for grid electricity, and spend-based (EEIO) factors for purchases you can't measure physically. Document every factor and its source — auditors and customers will ask.

Location-based vs market-based Scope 2

Report electricity two ways: location-based using the average grid intensity where you operate (see our country data), and market-based reflecting renewable contracts, certificates or supplier-specific factors you've bought.

Step 4 — Calculate, sense-check, publish

Sum by scope and category, then sense-check: emissions per employee for offices usually fall between 2 and 10 t CO₂e; if yours is far outside, look for unit errors. Our business estimator gives a quick benchmark to compare against.

Step 5 — Set a target and a plan

  • Switch to a renewable electricity contract or install on-site solar.
  • Electrify heating and fleet vehicles at replacement time.
  • Adopt a travel policy (rail under a set distance, fewer long-haul trips).
  • Engage your top 10 suppliers by spend on their own emissions.
  • Consider validating a near-term target with the Science Based Targets initiative's SME route.

Answering customer questionnaires

Be specific and honest: state the period, boundary, method, factors used and what's estimated. "We have measured Scope 1 and 2 and estimated Scope 3 using spend-based factors; we will move our top categories to supplier data next year" is a strong answer. A vague "we are carbon neutral" claim is a weak one.
